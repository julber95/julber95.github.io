I am passionate about machine learning and deep learning. What interests me is the principle itself: rather than
being specified by hand, a function is estimated from data, and its value lies in how well it generalises beyond
the training set. Deep networks extend this to high-dimensional inputs such as images, text or signals, by learning
their own representations of the data.

Health is the field where I find these methods most promising. Histopathology slides, medical images and clinical
records are high-dimensional, heterogeneous and costly to annotate. Models trained on such data could assist
specialists in diagnosis and treatment decisions, provided they are rigorously validated.

I am particularly interested in explainability. A model that performs well is a first result; understanding which
features it relies on, and how its internal representations lead to a given prediction, is a more complete one.
Attribution methods and faithfulness tests make it possible to check whether a model relies on relevant signal
rather than on spurious correlations, a prerequisite for any use in medicine.

Finally, it is a field in constant evolution. Architectures, training objectives and evaluation protocols change
quickly, and many questions on generalisation, robustness and interpretability remain open. This is what draws me
to research: formulating such questions precisely, designing experiments that can answer them, and contributing to
the progress of the field.
